From imagery to reviewed surveys.

A practical guide to mapping buildings, managing records, and preparing a drone survey.

SIH26012 · GeoVision

A five-minute demonstration

  1. On the hosted public demo, select Open SIH demo. No email or password is needed. Each visitor gets an isolated, temporary workspace, available for up to two hours. Use sample imagery and fictitious notes only; download your exports before the session expires or the service restarts.
  2. In Map, zoom to a clear residential area and draw a small scan rectangle around a few houses, no more than 500 × 500 m. Select Detect buildings. Detection uses the real segmentation engine; an empty result is possible. Satellite coverage, source quality and hosting capacity affect availability. Wait at least 15 seconds between scans.
  3. Select an outline, inspect the confidence and area, edit any incorrect vertices, and save a draft. After checking the source, mark it reviewed. The outline represents the roof footprint; draw or import a separate parcel when demonstrating land boundaries.
  4. Open Registry to download the survey PDF, CSV or GeoJSON. Demonstrate history after an edit and use overlap checks to identify conflicting saved boundaries. Demo reports are sample survey evidence and public snapshot verification is disabled.
  5. Open Drone, draw a convex scan area, preview the coverage route, and download its .plan. Hardware connections are disabled on the public host. Install locally to use compatible MAVLink telemetry; camera capture and physical flight remain ground-station workflows.

The hosted demo allows three projects per visitor, 200 saved features and four imagery sources per project. Each upload is limited to 8 MB and 16 million pixels; each imported-image scan uses at most 16 native windows. The larger limits described below apply to the local installation.

Present the actual results, missed buildings and corrections. Detection accuracy is not established without independent reference labels. This demo does not issue ownership documents or demonstrate a physically validated drone capture.

01 · Start a project

Keep each survey area together

  1. Sign in with your own email and password. On the first use, choose a password with at least 12 characters and enter your display name.
  2. Select a project in the header. Use ＋ to create one and Project settings to update its name, location, or purpose.
  3. Save your changes before signing out. Unsaved edits are recovered per account and project in this browser for up to seven days, within storage limits. If a recovery warning appears, keep the page open and save to the server.
02 · Map

Detect and review buildings

  1. Find the neighborhood using map search or the coordinate fields.
  2. Choose Draw scan area and mark a rectangle. Without a selection, detection uses the visible map.
  3. Choose Detect buildings. The default confidence threshold is 0.50 and minimum footprint area is 4 m². These filters can exclude small real structures.
  4. Select a boundary to inspect its area, confidence, and source warnings. Edit its vertices and add a name and notes.
  5. Use Save draft while checking the outline. Use Save reviewed after reviewing the geometry and source evidence.

AI roof outlines are building proposals. Use Draw parcel for a manually mapped land boundary; roof outlines do not establish ownership.

03 · Sources

Use your own imagery

  1. Open Sources and choose a GeoTIFF orthomosaic, PNG, or JPEG up to 64 MB.
  2. A georeferenced GeoTIFF supplies its own coordinate system and image transform.
  3. For PNG/JPEG, enter known west/south/east/north bounds. The image must be north-up and orthorectified. Do not guess its scale from browser zoom.
  4. Import the source, show its overlay, and select it for detection. New imports retain original image detail. Draw a rectangle inside large imagery to limit the scan to at most 256 native windows. Return to satellite imagery when you want to scan the map again.

Raw perspective drone photographs need photogrammetry and georeferencing before they can be measured on the map. This app imports the resulting orthomosaic.

04 · Drone

Connect telemetry and prepare a survey route

  1. Use a compatible MAVLink flight controller or a ground station that forwards MAVLink telemetry. DJI vendor SDK connections are not included.
  2. For UDP listener, enter the address of the computer running DroneMap AI and an unused port such as 14551. Configure the controller or telemetry router to send packets to that address and port. For a USB radio, refresh ports and select the detected serial device and matching baud rate. For TCP, enter the private address and port of the telemetry server.
  3. Select Connect vehicle. A real aircraft heartbeat is required for a connected state. Missing or lost heartbeats show waiting, timeout, or stale status.
  4. Draw a rectangular or convex survey area. Enter altitude, speed, overlap, and your actual camera specifications. The initial camera numbers are examples.
  5. Preview the route, then download the QGroundControl .plan. Review the aircraft/firmware settings, actual home elevation, complete flight sequence, and camera actions in your ground-control application.
  6. After capture and orthomosaic processing, import the georeferenced result through Sources and review its building detections.

The connection is read-only. This workspace does not arm, take off, upload a mission, trigger a camera, or execute a flight. The exported route contains waypoints only. Estimates assume flat ground and a nadir camera; terrain, obstacles, turn dynamics, battery reserve, and flight permissions need independent assessment.

05 · Registry

Keep the evidence usable

Search and filter saved surveys in Registry. Export GeoJSON for GIS work or CSV for tabular records. A survey PDF records the saved geometry and measurements, with a QR link to its versioned snapshot. History shows earlier saved revisions.

QR links share their report snapshot with anyone who has the link. Reports document survey evidence; they are not government-issued property titles.

Troubleshooting

If a scan or connection fails

No buildings: try clearer imagery or adjust confidence and minimum area. Results remain empty when no masks qualify.

AI unavailable: install the documented AI dependencies and confirm that best.pt is present.

Drone timeout: check telemetry forwarding, private IP, cable, port ownership, and baud rate. A UDP listener cannot make a controller start transmitting.

Unsaved edits: keep the page open and reconnect before saving. A version conflict means the saved feature changed elsewhere; reload it before continuing.

Quality and recovery

Check boundaries and retain your work

Registry Check overlaps identifies duplicate/overlapping saved boundaries within each feature type. Import parcels opens WGS84 GeoJSON references as unsaved drafts for review. Imports do not establish ownership.

Repeated scans are checked against saved and current buildings. Adjacent buildings and uncertain overlaps remain for manual review. GPS and battery values expire separately from the drone heartbeat.

Browser recovery has a 3 MiB / 1,000-boundary persistent limit. If storage is unavailable or work is larger, keep this page open and save to the workspace. Explicit sign-out discards current unsaved edits after confirmation.

The project README explains private backup/restore and the independent accuracy evaluation tool. Reference labels and a physical field test are still required for measured accuracy.
